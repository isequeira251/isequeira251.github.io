The pattern

Sales teams outgrow their HubSpot.

The portal was set up in a week, three years ago. Since then: nobody owns the records, activity lives in untracked notes, and every dashboard answers a slightly different question. Six things quietly break almost every portal I open — expand any of them:

Record ownership

Deals and companies with no owner get worked by nobody, and reporting by rep becomes fiction. The fix: assignment rules built from your actual territories, backfilled across the whole database in one pass, then kept current automatically.

Activity logging

Reps type real work into notes, where dashboards can't count it — I've seen held meetings undercounted five-fold. The fix: automation that converts notes into countable calls and meetings, so credit lands without changing how reps work.

Reporting dates

Created, booked, and occurred are three different dates, and most dashboards mix them freely. The fix: one agreed definition per report, enforced by properties instead of memory.

Pipeline design

Stages that describe hope ("proposal coming") instead of completed actions make every forecast a guess. The fix: stages gated on things that verifiably happened.

Segmentation

Without roles, tiers, or territories, every list is "everyone" and no campaign can be measured. The fix: scoring and classification written onto the records themselves, refreshed on a schedule.

Duplicates

Two records for one company split the relationship's history and double-count the pipeline. The fix: assisted dedup that merges the obvious and routes the judgment calls to a human.

FAQ

Things people ask before booking.

What actually happens in the free 30-minute audit?

A live screen-share look at your actual portal — read-only, no deck. We check ownership coverage, activity logging, pipeline structure, and reporting dates, and you leave with a prioritized fix list that's yours to keep whether or not we go further.

Do you need admin access?

Not to start — a view-only seat is enough for the audit. If we move into build work, access is scoped to what the work needs, and every bulk change runs as a dry run first so you see exactly what would change before it does.

Could you break something in our portal?

The whole practice is built around not doing that: audits never write, scripts report what they would change before they change anything, new workflows go live behind canary guards, and a daily monitor watches every job I ship. Nothing is enabled without your sign-off.

How does pricing work?

The 30-minute audit is free. Remediation work is quoted as a fixed scope from the audit's findings — you approve the number before anything starts — and ongoing help is a flat monthly retainer. No open-ended hourly meter.

We only have a handful of reps. Are we too small?

Sales-led teams without a dedicated CRM admin are exactly who this is for. The point is getting admin-grade infrastructure without hiring for it.

We already have an admin or an agency.

That works. Everything I build is documented and handed over in a state someone else can maintain — I'm comfortable being the API-and-automation layer alongside an in-house admin or an agency that owns the marketing side.
